02

Choose Your Focus

Choose 1 or 2 skills you wanted to improve this semester. Not sure? Look at your Self-Assessment and your Portfolio Build reflections. What did you say you wanted to work on?

  • Listening and note-taking
  • Reading for main ideas
  • Vocabulary and academic language
  • Summarizing, paraphrasing and using sources
  • Speaking and presenting
  • Reflection
  • Self-direction: planning and managing my work
  • Something else? Ask me first.

One clear focus is better than five. A narrow focus with strong evidence earns more than a broad focus with thin evidence.

03

Choose Your 5 Pieces

You choose. There are no fixed categories. The only question is: does this piece show my focus area?

1. All from EAP 100All five pieces are work you did in this course.
2. All show your focusEvery piece connects to your focus area.
3. One earlier, one laterInclude at least one earlier piece and one later piece. A before-and-after pair counts as one piece.
4. No speech videoThe Persuasive Speech video is not a portfolio artifact. Your slides, outline or notes are fine.

Ideas: Cornell notes, vocabulary sheets, reading responses, paragraphs with sources, slides, outlines, speaker notes, feedback you used, CCD reflections, resume drafts, DEIP reflections, revised drafts. The pieces you tracked in Portfolio Build 2 are a great place to start.

Five pieces is the minimum. Add more only if they help explain your growth.

8. Closing statementWhere you will use this skill next, in future study or work.100–150

DEIP for your focus area

  • Describe: Where did I start? Keep it short.
  • Explain: Why does this skill matter to me?
  • Interpret: What changed? Compare an earlier piece with a later piece. This part gets the most words.
  • Plan: What will I do next, and how will I check my progress?
05

Captions

One paragraph, 75–125 words, for every piece. Answer the same three questions each time:

  1. 01What is it?
  2. 02What does it show about my focus area?
  3. 03Why did I choose it?

“This is my … from Week … It shows my progress in … For example, … I chose it because …”

Sentence starters are optional. Use them, or write your own sentences.
