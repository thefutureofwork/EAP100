Student Q&A

Real questions from EAP 100 students, grouped by topic. Most answers point you to resources you already have. That is deliberate: part of becoming an independent learner is knowing where to look.

01

Planning, Energy, and Self-Management

Q1I am always too ambitious but lack the energy to follow through. How do I make a realistic plan?

This is the most common problem in university. Three things help:

  1. Write SMART goals. "Study more" is not a plan. "Complete Reading 5 guided notes, Saturday 10:00 to 11:30, in the library" is a plan. Task, time, place.
  2. Block 8 to 10 hours of homework per week in your calendar. Treat those blocks like class. If it is not scheduled, it will not happen.
  3. Look at your Week 2 time tracking. What actually took longest? Cut the tasks that waste time (recopying, formatting) and protect the ones that build skill (reading, writing, practicing).

Still struggling? Come to office hours (Tuesdays 13:00 to 14:15, N745) and we will build a weekly plan together.

Q2Besides SMART goals, what else helps?
  • Put a heading and a date on everything you submit. Week number, task name, date. Every single time. If I open your file and cannot tell what it is or when you wrote it, you have already lost marks for organization.
  • Label every upload to the Assignments folder clearly. Week number and task name in the file name, every time. A messy folder is a messy mind.
  • Track deadlines using the Checklist page or the calendar file.
  • Review notes after class, not the night before an exam.
  • Create a study group outside class time.
  • Stop procrastinating. Make better choices with your free time.
  • Take notes in class. Ask questions in class. Attend office hours.
  • Read assignment directions carefully before asking someone else.

None of this is complicated. All of it requires discipline.

Q3What is the key step in practicing SMART goals?

Writing them down and then doing them. A SMART goal you do not follow is just a sentence.

Q4I don't know what to do right now.

Do not put the cart before the horse. You do not need to understand the whole semester to do this week's tasks. Open the weekly schedule. Look at Week 4. Do the first task you have not finished.

If you are confused about what a task requires, read the assignment directions on the website carefully. If you are still confused after that, email me at kt1992@nyu.edu.

Confused? Do what you can. I will teach you the skills you need. You need to keep up with the coursework. Do not use confusion as an excuse to do nothing.

02

Language Accuracy and Speaking

Q5How can I improve my language accuracy?

There is no shortcut, but there are habits that work:

  • Read more English. Course readings count, but go beyond the course too.
  • Listen to more English. Podcasts, TED talks, lectures. Immerse yourself.
  • Write every week and review your own writing after you receive feedback.
  • Book an ARC appointment. An EAP specialist will work on your specific errors with you.
  • Use the EAP Foundation website (opens in a new tab). Grammar, vocabulary, and academic writing exercises, all free.
Q6How can I improve my spoken English more efficiently?

Speak more English. That is the honest answer. Discuss readings with classmates in English. Practice your presentation out loud, not in your head. Come to office hours and talk through your ideas. Use CCD visits as speaking practice: ask questions, introduce yourself to professionals. The more you use it, the faster it improves.

Q7How do I improve listening and writing at the same time?

This is exactly what Cornell notes train. Stop trying to write everything. Listen for signposting phrases ("The main point is...", "There are three reasons...") and capture those. The Cornell Notes template (opens in a new tab) separates listening from processing. Practice with Videos 1 through 4 before the exam.

The library is also running "Note-Taking: From Ears to Action" (Wed Oct 28, 3:45 to 4:45 PM).

Q8How can I make my handwriting better?

Write more by hand. Slow down until clarity catches up with speed. If your handwriting is genuinely illegible on an exam, print in block letters.

03

Sources, Citations, and APA

Q9How do I use APA citation style?

The Templates & Tools (opens in a new tab) document has an APA quick reference. The basic pattern:

  • In-text: (Author, Year) or Author (Year) states that...
  • Reference list: Author, A. B. (Year). Title of work. Publisher.

The library ran an APA workshop in Week 3. If you missed it, book time with a librarian. They will walk you through it.

Q10Why is sourcing so important? Just for reliability?

Reliability is part of it. But sourcing also means giving credit to the person whose idea you are using, letting your reader check your claim, and showing that your argument rests on evidence rather than opinion. An unsourced claim is an unfinished claim. Look at how every reading in the Reading Packet (opens in a new tab) uses citations and practice doing the same.

Q11Why cite the source if the source is the passage I was given?

Because citing correctly is a skill you are building. The reader needs to know which part of the passage you mean. Name the author and year even when the source feels obvious. Practice now so it becomes automatic later.

04

Portfolio and DEIP

Q12How can I make my portfolio better?
  1. Put a heading and a date on every artifact. Week number, task name, date. No exceptions.
  2. Choose artifacts that show growth, not just completion. A revised paragraph is more interesting than a first draft.
  3. Write stronger DEIP reflections. Describe, Explain, Interpret, Plan. Most students forget Plan.

Full structure is in the Portfolio Assignment Guide (opens in a new tab).

Q13I have difficulty connecting DEIP reflections to EAP 100 skills.

Open the Student Guide (opens in a new tab) and look at the five learning outcomes. Every artifact connects to at least one. In your Interpret section, name the outcome explicitly: "This reading task helped me practice selecting relevant information (Outcome 2) because I had to decide which details supported the author's main claim."

Q14What skills are linked to interpreting artifacts in the portfolio?

Self-assessment, connecting your work to course outcomes, identifying strengths and weaknesses, and planning next steps. These are the same skills employers test when they ask "Tell me about a time you improved at something."

05

Course Content and What Comes Next

Q15What will we learn in future classes?

Open the weekly schedule. Every week is listed with its topic, tasks, and resources. Do not worry about Week 10 right now. Focus on this week and next.

Q16Is EAP 100 just academic English skills?

No. EAP 100 teaches you to read critically, listen selectively, speak with evidence, write from sources, and present yourself professionally. These skills transfer directly into every course you take and every job you hold. Read the Student Guide (opens in a new tab) introduction again.

Q17How can we learn about work from TED talks recorded years ago?

The TED talks are not instructions. They are sources: arguments made by researchers at a specific point in time. Your job is to evaluate those arguments, not to accept them. When Brynjolfsson (2013) talks about the second machine age, ask: has his prediction held up? That critical evaluation is the skill.

Q18Are Cornell notes meant to capture all the details?

No. Main ideas and key supporting details, not every word. Right column: notes during the lecture. Left column: questions and keywords added afterward. Bottom: summary you actually study from. See the Cornell Notes template (opens in a new tab).

06

Listening Exam Preparation

Q19How do I prepare for the listening test? I can't recall all the sources from Weeks 1 through 5.

You do not need to memorize every source. You need to:

  1. Sit the Practice Listening Exam under real conditions (due Mon Sep 28). One viewing. No pausing.
  2. Review your Cornell notes from Videos 1 through 4. Know the main argument of each talk.
  3. Practice separating main points from supporting details. Reading 6 trains this.
  4. Know 30+ academic vocabulary words from Weeks 1 through 4.
  5. Be able to write a 3 to 5 sentence summary after a single listen.

Section C asks you to connect the exam lecture to one course source. Pick the two sources you know best and review those.

07

Midterm Presentation

Q20How do I make a boring but important topic interesting?

Start with a question your audience cares about, not a definition. Use a real example, a surprising number, or a short story. Show visuals that explain, not decorate. Speak to your audience, not at your slides.

Q21Where do I put my midterm presentation draft?

Student Folder, labeled "Week 4 Midterm Presentation First Draft." Upload your outline and slides. Due Mon Sep 28.
