Student Guide

Course overview, learning outcomes, Future-Ready framing, and quick-reference reminders

When you need it: Week 1, and any time you are unsure what the course is asking of you.

Big Course Question

What will work look like in the future, and how can I prepare for it?

You will explore this question through readings, listening texts, class discussions, career research, workplace inquiry, simulations, portfolio tasks, and presentations. This question is not only about jobs. It is also about communication, identity, learning, ethics, technology, and the choices you make as you prepare for academic and professional life.

Course Overview

EAP 100: Future Focused is a first-year English for Academic Purposes course that uses the future of work as the thematic vehicle for academic English development. The course emphasizes speaking and listening, while also building reading, writing, source use, reflection, and presentation skills through readings, videos, CCD engagement, portfolio work, and structured reflection.

Official EAP 100 Learning Outcomes

  1. Prepare and deliver short presentations on course topics with coherence, grammatical accuracy, critical thinking, and appropriate academic/concept vocabulary.
  2. Select relevant information from primary and secondary sources, including recorded lectures, talks, and other multimedia.
  3. Summarize, paraphrase, and quote from a variety of sources, including appropriate source reference in oral work.
  4. Synthesize relevant information from sources with personal ideas and reflections.
  5. Interact effectively and respectfully with classmates in discussion, pair work, group work, and question-and-answer settings.

Future-Ready Framing

EAP 100 is grounded in Future-Ready Competencies drawn from major international education and workforce frameworks. In this course those competencies support, not replace, the core goals of academic English development. As you explore the future of work, you strengthen all seven:

  1. Critical, analytical and creative thinking
  2. Self-direction, adaptability and learning-to-learn
  3. Collaboration, communication and empathy
  4. Ethics, responsibility and citizenship
  5. Digital and technological fluency
  6. Sustainability and global / planetary awareness
  7. Resilience, wellbeing, and acting under uncertainty

Your portfolio is where that development becomes visible — to you, and to a future employer.

Quick-Reference Reminders

🎧 Listening task reminder: every listening task includes Cornell-style notes, 5–10 academic vocabulary words, main ideas and supporting details, and a short summary or takeaway.
📝 DEIP reflection reminder: Describe (What did I do?) → Explain (Why was this important in EAP 100?) → Interpret (What did I learn?) → Plan (What will I change next time?)
📂 Portfolio reminder: keep copies of portfolio-related work as you complete it, and add short reflective notes explaining why an artifact matters. The Student Showcase is Thu Dec 10; the Final Portfolio is due Sun Dec 13.
