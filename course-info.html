Portfolio

Your portfolio has two lives. The Working Portfolio is your evidence bank from Weeks 1 to 13: developmental, cumulative, and not expected to look polished. The Final Showcase Portfolio is the curated version you submit on Dec 13 — smaller, more selective, and making an argument about your growth.

Two deliverables, one portfolio. You submit the full curated portfolio (five artifacts, A–E, plus reflections). You present only two or three of them at the Student Showcase on Thu Dec 10.

Six folders. Save meaningful work as you go and label it clearly.

Five artifacts is the minimum. Add more only if they strengthen the argument.

Every caption answers three things

  1. What is this artifact?
  2. What skill does it demonstrate?
  3. Why did you select it?

How it is graded

  • Selection. Judgment, not volume.
  • Captions. You can say why an artifact matters.
  • Evidence of growth. The portfolio clearly shows academic English development.
  • Coherence. One shaped presentation, not a folder of unrelated tasks.
  • Reflection quality. The major DEIP interprets growth, it does not just describe tasks.
  • Audience awareness. The final product is clear to an outside reader.
The most common mistake. Describing what you did (“I wrote notes”) instead of interpreting what it shows (“My notes reveal that I can identify main ideas but miss supporting detail”). Every DEIP reflection needs all four parts, especially Plan.

Policies

Attendance

You have 2 free absences, no explanation needed. Three absences means a participation deduction. Four means an F for participation. Five means course failure. If you know in advance, notify me before class. For any absence, check with a classmate about what was covered.

Late work

5% deducted per day. Not accepted after 7 days. If you have extenuating circumstances, email me before the due date. An absence does not excuse a late submission.

Deadlines

Weekly reading, listening and reflection tasks are due at 23:59 on the Monday after the week they belong to, unless the task says otherwise. Portfolio builds and CCD logs sometimes fall earlier in the week, so always check the date on the task itself.

AI use

AI use is task-specific and sometimes restricted. I will tell you when it is permitted. Submitting AI-generated content as your own work is never allowed. When AI use is permitted, include an AI disclosure statement. Full details are in the Syllabus.

FAQ

Q1Where do I submit my work?

Three routes, and the task always tells you which one:

1. Student Folder on the course Google Drive — almost everything. Label it with the week, for example “Week 1 Reading 1 Paragraph”.
2. On paper, in class — a few tasks like the Planning Guide and the resume peer review.
3. Brightspace — the three CCD online modules only.

Always keep a copy of portfolio-related work in your own portfolio folders.

Q2When exactly is something due?

Open the weekly schedule and find the task. Every task has its own date chip. The general rule is 23:59 on the Monday after the week, but portfolio builds and CCD logs often fall on a Thursday instead.

Q3How much time should this take?

Plan for 8–10 hours per week outside class. That includes reading, listening practice, exam revision and speech rehearsal. Your Week 2 time-tracking task gives you a personal baseline. If you are consistently over, come to office hours — that is useful information for both of us.

Q4I missed a class. What now?

Check with a classmate about what was covered, then open the week on the schedule for the tasks. Missing class does not extend a deadline. You have 2 free absences before it affects your participation grade.

Q5Can I use AI for my assignments?

Sometimes, and I will tell you when. It is task-specific. Submitting AI-generated content as your own work is never allowed. When AI use is permitted, include an AI disclosure statement. See the Syllabus for the full policy.

Q6What is DEIP?

The reflection model used in every portfolio build: Describe (what did I do?) → Explain (why did it matter in EAP 100?) → Interpret (what did I learn?) → Plan (what will I change next time?). All four parts, every time. Plan is the one people forget.

Q7What does a listening task actually include?

Every listening task has four parts: Cornell-style notes, 5–10 academic vocabulary words with brief meanings, main ideas plus supporting details, and a short summary or takeaway. The template is in Templates & Tools.

Q8How do I book office hours?

Walk-in Tuesdays 13:00–14:15 in Room N745, no booking needed. For any other time, book a slot at least 24 hours ahead. The Week 8 individual meeting has a separate booking link.
