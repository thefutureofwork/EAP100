Practice Exam 1 Answers

Mark scheme and model answers for the Practice Listening Exam. Do not open this page until you have completed the paper independently.

A

Section A: Discrete Items

16 marks
A11True / False
McAfee accepts that people have been predicting technology would destroy employment for about 200 years, and that they have always been wrong.
Answer
TRUE. He concedes the point directly before arguing why this time is different.
1 mark for TRUE.
A22
According to McAfee, what makes the present moment different from earlier waves of automation?
Answer
Machines have demonstrated skills they never had before: reading, writing, speaking, listening, translating. The work being automated is now cognitive, not only physical.
1 mark for “new skills machines never had before.” 1 mark for naming the cognitive/mental character of that work, or for two concrete skills.
A34
McAfee names three technologies that are displacing specific kinds of work. Give TWO, and state the job each one affects.
Answer
Any two of: (1) autonomous / self-driving cars → truck drivers; (2) Siri connected to Watson → customer service workers; (3) warehouse robots → people who work the shelves.
2 marks per correct pairing (1 technology + 1 job). No mark for a technology without its job.
A41
What name do McAfee and Erik Brynjolfsson give to the period we are now in?
Answer
The New Machine Age.
Accept “new machine age” or “second machine age.”
A51True / False
McAfee says that corporate profits and total wages paid out in the economy are both at an all-time high.
Answer
FALSE. Returns to capital (corporate profits) are at an all-time high; returns to labor (total wages) are at an all-time low.
1 mark for FALSE.
A61
Complete Reuther’s reply: “Hey Henry, how are you going to get them to …”
Answer
“…buy cars?”
1 mark. Accept any wording that captures robots as consumers.
A71
What has happened to median income in America over the past 15 years?
Answer
It has gone down.
1 mark for “declined / fallen / gone down.” No mark for “stayed flat.”
A84
McAfee tells a story about two workers, Ted and Bill. Describe each in one sentence.
Answer
Ted is a college-educated professional (manager, engineer, doctor, lawyer) at the top of the American middle class. Bill has no college education and does low-level white-collar or blue-collar work.
2 marks each. 1 mark for education level, 1 mark for kind of work or class position.
A91
Name ONE way, other than employment, in which Bill’s and Ted’s lives have diverged since 1960.
Answer
Any one of: marriage stability; whether their children grew up in a two-parent home; voting in presidential elections; likelihood of going to prison.
1 mark for any one of the four.
B

Section B: Short Comprehension

12 marks
B14
McAfee quotes Voltaire: “Work saves a man from three great evils: boredom, vice and need.” Explain why he uses this quotation at this point in the talk.
Answer
He uses Voltaire right after describing Bill’s decline. Work is not only income; it is structure, status, and purpose. The three evils map onto what Bill has lost. This connects to McAfee’s claim that managing the transition is society’s greatest challenge.
1 mark: identifies the quote as following the Bill/Ted divergence. 1 mark: explains work as more than income. 1 mark: links the three evils to Bill’s specific decline. 1 mark: connects it to McAfee’s larger claim about the transition.
B24
McAfee says: “I’m going to start worrying about those the day my computer becomes aware of my printer.” What is he dismissing, and what is he doing rhetorically?
Answer
He is dismissing the sci-fi fear of self-aware machines. The joke clears the distraction so the audience focuses on inequality and middle-class job loss. It also positions him as evidence-driven rather than alarmist.
1 mark: names the dystopian/self-aware-machine fear. 1 mark: identifies humor or dismissal as the device. 1 mark: explains the purpose (redirect to real challenges). 1 mark: comments on how it builds his credibility or stance.
B34
McAfee insists the coming abundance is “not shallow materialism.” Explain the distinction he is drawing.
Answer
“Shallow materialism” means wanting more things. McAfee means prices fall, quality rises, poverty decreases, and drudgery disappears. He makes the distinction to preempt the objection that he is just celebrating consumption.
1 mark: defines shallow materialism. 1 mark: explains what McAfee means by abundance instead. 1 mark: names the anticipated objection. 1 mark: explains why the distinction matters to his argument.
C

Section C: Synthesis

12 marks
C112
In 200–250 words, connect McAfee’s argument to ONE other source from Weeks 1–5. State McAfee’s central claim accurately; state the other source’s position; identify one point of agreement AND one point of genuine tension; and refer to both sources properly.
What the answer must include
McAfee’s central claim: The New Machine Age is different because machines now do cognitive work, creating abundance but breaking the link between work and income for less-educated workers.

Second source: Any source from Weeks 1–5. The exemplar uses Rainer Strack (“The Workforce Crisis of 2030”), who predicts a global labor shortage from falling birth rates and aging populations.

Agreement: Both speakers agree the labor market of the future will not look like the present one, and both argue education systems must change.

Tension: If Strack is right that workers will be scarce, then McAfee’s fear of a jobless underclass should not happen. A shortage of workers overall can coexist with a surplus of workers whose particular skills have been automated.

Award marks for: accurate central claim (2), accurate second source (2), genuine agreement (2), genuine tension with analysis (4), proper source references (2). Dates are not required in citations for this listening exam.
