NYU Shanghai · English for Academic Purposes · Fall 2026

Working Portfolio

Purpose

The Working Portfolio is your course workspace. You save selected work here as the semester continues. It is developmental, cumulative, and reflective. It is not expected to look polished at every stage.

It exists to help you collect evidence, track growth, and prepare for the Final Showcase Portfolio.

💡 This is your evidence bank, not your final product. Save meaningful work, label it clearly, and keep enough reflection that you can choose strong artifacts later.

What goes in it

  • Selected reading responses and source-based paragraphs
  • Cornell listening notes and vocabulary lists
  • Presentation outlines, slides, cue cards, and speaker notes
  • CCD visit reflections and career planning materials
  • DEIP reflections
  • Portfolio Build 1, 2, and 3 materials
  • Revisions, feedback notes, and planning materials
  • Final reflection and closing statement drafts

Final Showcase Portfolio

Purpose

The Final Showcase Portfolio is your curated final product. It is smaller, more selective, and more audience-aware than the Working Portfolio. The Working Portfolio stores evidence; this one makes an argument about your growth.

🎤 Two deliverables, one portfolio. You submit the full curated portfolio (five artifacts, A–E). You present only two or three of them at the Student Showcase.
DeliverableWhenScope
Student Showcase talk (required)Thu Dec 10, Week 142–3 artifacts you present and explain aloud
Final Portfolio submission (10%)Sun Dec 13, before midnightThe full curated portfolio: all 8 sections below

Required structure

SectionWhat it includesLength
1. Portfolio introductionWho you are as a learner and communicator, and what this portfolio shows.150–200 words
2. Artifact A: Reading / source useOne strong reading-based or source-based artifact.Artifact + 75–125 word caption
3. Artifact B: Listening / note-takingCornell notes, vocabulary sheet, or listening response.Artifact + 75–125 word caption
4. Artifact C: SpeakingPresentation slides, outline, or speaker notes.Artifact + 75–125 word caption
5. Artifact D: CCD / experientialOne CCD visit reflection or career planning artifact.Artifact + 75–125 word caption
6. Artifact E: Reflective growthThe artifact that best shows growth, revision, or self-direction.Artifact + 75–125 word caption
7. Major DEIP reflectionOne substantial reflection that pulls the whole portfolio together.300–500 words
8. Future-facing closing statementWhere this takes you in future study and work.100–150 words

Five artifacts is the minimum. Add more only if they strengthen the argument.

Caption format

Every artifact gets a short caption. Answer three things:

  1. What it is
  2. What skill it shows
  3. Why you selected it

Major DEIP reflection

It should pull the portfolio together, explain your growth across the course, name what challenged you, name what you improved, and identify what you carry forward.

Future-facing closing statement

Connect the portfolio to future study and to professional communication. Help an outside reader understand what you can now do, and end with a clear sense of direction.

The Showcase talk

On Thu Dec 10 you present two to three artifacts from your curated set, not the whole portfolio. Choose the ones that best tell your growth story out loud. You rehearse these in Week 13 with peer feedback.

How it is assessed

What is assessedWhy it matters
Quality of artifact selectionRewards judgment, not volume.
Quality of captions and explanationYou should be able to say why an artifact matters.
Evidence of EAP skill developmentThe portfolio should clearly show academic English growth.
Coherence as a wholeOne shaped presentation, not a folder of unrelated tasks.
Quality of reflectionThe major DEIP should interpret growth, not just describe tasks.
Presentation and audience awarenessThe final product should be clear to an outside reader.
