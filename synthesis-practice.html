Will AI take your job?

250–300 words. Time how long it takes to write your answer. Watch the talk, plan with the writing frame, write your answer, then compare it with the two color-coded samples and grade it with the guide.

▶Vinciane Beauchêne: Will AI take your job in the next 10 years? Wrong questionTED@BCG · October 202512 min (opens in a new tab)
See Week 8 schedule
Your writing time00:00

Press Start when you begin writing. Press Stop when you finish your last sentence.

Synthesis question

  • The central claim
  • 1 Weeks 1–8 source (author, year)
  • 2–3 quotes from your quote sheet, in quotation marks, with APA citations, each explained
  • One agreement
  • One genuine tension
  • Your own evaluation
  • Two examples of the speaker’s own opinion

Primary and secondary sources required.

Color key

🟦 Central claim 🟪 Secondary source 🟩 Primary source 🩷 Speaker’s opinion 🟨 Agreement 🟧 Tension 🟥 Evaluation

Writing frame

🟦 Central claimAI will [main effect on jobs or tasks], but [your qualification or condition]. Overall, the evidence suggests [your answer to the question].
🟪 Secondary source[Author or organization]’s ([year]) [report/article title] is a secondary source because it [analyzes, summarizes, or interprets evidence]. It states, “[exact quote from your quote sheet]” ([Author/Organization], [year], p. [page]). This means [explain the quote in your own words] and supports/challenges my claim because [why it matters].
🟩 Primary source · 🩷 opinion 1In [speaker’s name]’s ([year]) [talk/interview/video], the speaker gives a personal view: “[exact quote showing opinion 1]” ([Speaker], [year], [timestamp]). This is the speaker’s opinion that [explain the opinion], rather than a proven fact because [brief reason].
🟩 Primary source · 🩷 opinion 2The speaker also argues, “[exact quote showing opinion 2]” ([Speaker], [year], [timestamp]). This expresses the view that [explain the second opinion]. It matters because [connect it to your claim].
🟨 AgreementThe two sources agree that [shared point]. The secondary source shows this by [evidence or idea], while the speaker emphasizes [related idea].
🟧 TensionHowever, the sources differ about [specific disagreement or difference in emphasis]. While [source 1’s position], [source 2’s position]. This is a genuine tension because [explain why the ideas do not fully match].
🟥 EvaluationI find [source/argument] more convincing because [your reason and evidence]. However, [limitation of the evidence or source]. Therefore, I conclude that [your final answer about AI and jobs].

Sample 1

Color-coded model answer
🟦 Central claimAI is likely to transform many jobs and replace some tasks, but whether workers lose their jobs will depend partly on how organizations redesign work and prepare employees.
🟪 Secondary sourceThe World Economic Forum (WEF), a secondary source based on employers’ expectations, reports that “employers expect 39% of workers’ core skills to change by 2030” (World Economic Forum, 2025, “Expected disruptions to skills” section). This forecast suggests a major shift in what workers need to know, but it does not mean that 39% of jobs will disappear.
🟩 Primary source · 🩷 two opinionsIn her TED talk, leadership expert Vinciane Beauchêne shares her professional perspective. She says, “I believe the test was wrong” (Beauchêne, 2025, 00:20), challenging the idea that human-like conversation is the best measure of intelligence. Her second opinion is that “there is no universal list” of human qualities that AI cannot replace (Beauchêne, 2025, 06:43). She argues that each organization must identify where people make a meaningful difference.
🟨 AgreementBoth sources suggest that work will change and people need to prepare. The WEF highlights shifting skill demands, while Beauchêne advocates training and new role designs.
🟧 TensionThe WEF describes expected changes in skills across the workforce, whereas Beauchêne warns that AI agents may perform entire work processes. Her warning is more dramatic, but it is supported mainly by client examples rather than broad evidence.
🟥 EvaluationBeauchêne’s argument is persuasive because organizations should not expect workers to adapt without support. However, neither source proves exactly how many jobs AI will eliminate. The WEF reports forecasts, and Beauchêne presents a leadership perspective. I conclude that AI will replace some tasks and reshape some jobs; training and job design will influence who benefits from the change.

Sample 2

Color-coded model answer
🟦 Central claimAI is likely to change many jobs, but whether it replaces workers or reshapes their roles depends on how organizations use technology and prepare employees.
🟪 Secondary sourceThe Week 4 reading presents the World Economic Forum’s Future of Jobs Report, which summarizes employers’ expectations. It reports, “employers expect 39% of workers’ core skills to change by 2030” (World Economic Forum, 2025, Section 3.1). This predicts skill change, not 39% job loss.
🟩 Primary source · 🩷 two opinionsBeauchêne’s TED talk is a primary source. 🩷 First, she says, “I believe the test was wrong” (Beauchêne, 2025, 00:20), challenging the idea that conversation alone proves intelligence. 🩷 She also says, “I believe we have real hard work to do if we want this narrative to stay true” (Beauchêne, 2025, 03:35). She means AI augmentation is not guaranteed; leaders must act to make it true.
🟨 AgreementBoth sources agree work and required skills are changing. However, the Week 4 reading explains that many roles change in content rather than disappear, while Beauchêne warns agents may complete whole processes, such as selling a product.
🟧 TensionThis raises a genuine question: will AI mainly alter tasks, or eliminate some roles?
🟥 EvaluationBeauchêne’s warning is persuasive because her sales example shows automation can shift human work toward relationships. Still, one client example cannot predict every industry, and the WEF report records forecasts, not outcomes. I conclude that AI will replace some tasks and reshape jobs, but the scale of job loss depends on organizational choices, worker training, and whether new roles emerge.

Grading guide

GradeWhat the response demonstrates
ASpecific source ideas are clearly explained and interact insightfully. Agreement and tension are meaningful; evaluation adds a thoughtful judgment.
A−Source ideas are explained and connected importantly, though the interaction has less insight or depth.
B+Ideas are well explained and sources mostly interact, but the analysis lacks some depth.
BIdeas are understandable; source interaction is basic or partly developed.
B−Mostly general explanation, with minimal or obvious connection between sources.
C+ to C−Attempts synthesis, but connections are incomplete, unclear, or include significant misunderstanding.
D / FD: little or no interaction between sources. F: no actual attempt to answer.

Also consider: Does the writing stay in the student’s own words? Are citations complete and appropriate? Do language or organization make the synthesis hard to understand?
