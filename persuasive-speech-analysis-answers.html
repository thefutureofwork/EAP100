Show Your Ability: Answers

Check both Week 9 tasks on Jason Shen’s TED talk. Factual questions have clear answers. Opinion questions show one strong example, not the only right answer.

Back to the task page
How to use this page. Compare it with what you submitted. Then write down one correction to your analysis and one improvement to a claim, and add both to your Speech Planner. Do not copy the examples into your own speech: your wording must be yours.

Task 1: Model Speech Analysis

A. First viewing

A1What is the problem with hiring?

Employers look at the wrong things, like degrees, job titles and famous schools. So they miss people with real ability.

A2His three ideas ★ Key

  1. Expand your search.
  2. Hire for performance.
  3. Get the bigger picture.

A3Finish the sentence

Shen argues that employers should judge people by what they can do, not just by their experience or degree.

B. Second viewing

B1How does he start?

With a question: You know who I’m envious of? It makes us curious.

B2The Etsy story

He had never been a product manager, but he built a website analyzing Etsy, with ideas for new features, and he got the job. It shows that showing your skills can matter more than a job title.

B3One example for each idea

  • Idea 1: the Oakland Athletics baseball team chose players other teams ignored.
  • Idea 2: give a data analyst a spreadsheet and ask for their key insights.
  • Idea 3: his kindergarten teacher misjudged him because she did not know his family situation.

B4What he asks at the end

Employers: stop using old hiring methods and look for ability. Job seekers: tell your story in a powerful way.

C. Check the evidence ★ Key

C1His Etsy story

Shows: one person got a job by showing his skills.
Cannot prove: that this would work for everyone.

C2The 2010 study

Shows: many graduates work in jobs unrelated to their degree.
Cannot prove: that this is still true today, or true in every country. He also does not name the study, so we cannot check it.

Check yours: did you finish the “cannot prove” half? That is the part that matters most. A story or an old study is real evidence, but it has limits.

D. How does he persuade?

EthosD1

We trust Shen because he changed careers himself and has studied hiring. But he started a hiring company, so he may benefit if employers agree with him.

PathosD2

The kindergarten story makes me feel sympathy or sadness, because a young child was almost labeled unfairly. It supports his point that quick judgments can be wrong. Other feelings are fine if your “because” explains them.

LogosD3

He says employers should test skills because doing a real task shows what a person can actually do.

E. What could someone say against him?

E1One problem with hiring tasks

Any of the three works with a clear sentence. Unpaid work: companies could make applicants do a lot of work for free. Too much time: long tasks are hard for people with jobs or families. Unfair: some people have less time, money or support to do them well.

E2Does Shen answer it?

No. He does not talk about pay, time or fairness for applicants.

E3Your fix ★ Key

ExampleMy idea is that employers should keep tasks short, give a time limit, and pay for long tasks.

Your fix can be different. Check that it answers the problem you chose in E1.

F. Your opinion ★ Key

F180–100 words: did it persuade you?

Example (92 words)The talk is persuasive because Shen uses his own Etsy story, so the audience can easily understand his idea. His three numbered ideas also make the talk easy to follow. One weakness is that he does not explain his research, such as the 2010 study, so I cannot check it. He also does not talk about unpaid work. He could make it stronger by naming his sources and explaining how to keep hiring tasks short and fair. Overall, I think he shows that ability matters, but not how to measure it fairly.

Check yours: 80–100 words? All four sentence starters? One real weakness, not only praise? You may disagree with this example.

F2A technique to copy

For example: numbering my main ideas, or starting with a question.

F3My speech claim

ExampleUniversities should give students credit for career workshops because these workshops build skills for future jobs.

Yours should follow the same pattern: [Who] should [do what] because [reason].

F4Evidence I still need

ExampleEvidence that students who attend workshops feel more prepared to apply for jobs.

Task 2: Language Review

A. Three persuasive phrases

PhraseWhat it does
A1You know who I’m envious of?Makes us curious
A2One: expand your search.Organizes ideas
A3So let’s go out and build it.Asks us to act

B. Make claims more careful ★ Key

Any accurate version that uses a word from the box is correct.

B1“Degrees tell employers nothing about ability.”

Degrees do not necessarily show every ability a person has.

B2“Work samples always find the best person.”

Work samples can help employers find good candidates, but they do not always find the best person.

B3“AI will replace all jobs.”

AI may replace some jobs. / AI may change many jobs.

Check yours: did you actually make the claim weaker and more accurate? Changing only one word, like “all” to “every,” does not count.

C. Five sentences ★ Key

Your sentences are about your own topic, so there is no single answer. Here is a model.

Example
  1. I argue that universities should give first-year students credit for career workshops.
  2. My first reason is that these workshops teach useful skills, like writing a resume.
  3. Some people may say that this adds too much work for students.
  4. However, the workshops can replace some other assignments.
  5. So I recommend that universities make the workshops short and connected to courses.

Check yours: does sentence 4 use a careful word such as may or can?

D. Say it aloud

D1Pauses and key words

ExampleI argue / that universities should give credit / for career workshops.

Any sentence with sensible pauses and 2–3 key words underlined is correct. D2 is speaking only.

E. Reflect

Answers will vary. Examples:

  • E1: “AI will replace all jobs” → “AI may change many jobs.”
  • E2: I spoke more slowly and paused after my claim.
  • E3: Stressing my key words more clearly.
For your speech on Nov 24: take your best careful claim from Part B or C and use it in your counterargument response. Then practice it aloud with pauses, like D1.
