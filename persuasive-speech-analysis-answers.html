Task 1: Model Speech AnalysisJason Shen · Looking for a Job? Highlight Your Ability, Not Your Experience · TED, 2017

A. First viewing: the big message

A1What is the problem with hiring?

Employers look at the wrong things, like degrees, job titles and famous schools. So they miss people with real ability.

A2What are his three ideas? ★ Key

  1. Expand your search.
  2. Hire for performance.
  3. Get the bigger picture.

A3Finish the sentence.

Shen argues that employers should judge people by what they can do, not just by their experience or degree.

B. Second viewing: the details

B1How does he start the talk?

With a question: You know who I'm envious of? It makes us curious.

B2What happened when he applied to Etsy? What does the story show?

He had never been a product manager, but he built a website analyzing Etsy with ideas for new features, and he got the job. It shows that showing your skills can matter more than a job title.

B3One example for each idea

  • Idea 1: The Oakland Athletics baseball team chose players other teams ignored.
  • Idea 2: Give a data analyst a spreadsheet and ask for their key insights.
  • Idea 3: His kindergarten teacher misjudged him because she did not know his family situation.

B4What does he ask employers and job seekers to do?

Employers: stop using old hiring methods and look for ability. Job seekers: tell their story in a powerful way.

C. Check the evidence ★ Key

C1His Etsy story

This story shows that one person got a job by showing his skills. But it cannot prove that this would work for everyone.

C2The 2010 study about graduates’ jobs and degrees

This study shows that many graduates work in jobs unrelated to their degree. But it cannot prove that this is still true today, or true in every country. (He does not name the study.)

For your speech: say what your evidence shows, and be honest about what it cannot show. A careful audience trusts you more, not less.

D. How does he persuade?

D1Ethos Trust

We trust Shen because he changed careers himself and has studied hiring. But he started a hiring company, so he may benefit if employers agree with him.

D2Pathos Feelings

The kindergarten story makes me feel sympathy or sadness, because a young child was almost labeled unfairly. It supports his point that quick judgments can be wrong.

Other feelings are fine if you explain why.

D3Logos Reasons

He says employers should test skills because doing a real task shows what a person can actually do.

E. What could someone say against him?

E1One problem with hiring tasks

Unpaid work: companies could make applicants do a lot of work for free.

Also correct: it takes too much time for people with jobs or families, or it is unfair to some people.

E2Does Shen answer this problem?

No. He does not talk about pay, time or fairness for applicants.

E3How would you fix it? ★ Key

My idea is that employers should keep tasks short, give a time limit, and pay for long tasks.

Any fix is fine if it really solves the problem you chose in E1.

For your speech: this is exactly what your counterargument needs. Name a fair problem, then answer it. Shen skipped this step. You don’t have to.

F. Your opinion ★ Key

F1Did the talk persuade you? (80–100 words)

Sample answer · 92 words

The talk is persuasive because Shen uses his own Etsy story, so the audience can easily understand his idea. His three numbered ideas also make the talk easy to follow. One weakness is that he does not explain his research, such as the 2010 study, so I cannot check it. He also does not talk about unpaid work. He could make it stronger by naming his sources and explaining how to keep hiring tasks short and fair. Overall, I think he shows that ability matters, but not how to measure it fairly.

Your opinion can be different. A strong answer uses all four sentence starters and gives a reason for each point.

F2One thing I will copy in my own speech

Numbering my main ideas, or starting with a question.

F3My speech claim (sample)

Universities should give students credit for career workshops because these workshops build skills for future jobs.

F4One piece of evidence I still need (sample)

Evidence that students who attend workshops feel more prepared to apply for jobs.

Next step: bring your F3 claim to class. You will test it with the Claim Doctor in the Persuasive Speech Planner.

Task 2: Language ReviewPersuasive phrases · careful claims · your five sentences

A. Find three persuasive phrases

PhraseWhat it does

A2One: expand your search.Organizes ideas
A3So let's go out and build it.Asks us to act

B. Make claims more careful ★ Key

Any accurate version that uses a word from the box is correct.

B1“Degrees tell employers nothing about ability.”

Degrees do not necessarily show every ability a person has.

B2“Work samples always find the best person.”

Work samples can help employers find good candidates, but they do not always find the best person.

B3“AI will replace all jobs.”

AI may replace some jobs. or AI may change many jobs.

C. Write five sentences ★ Key

Sample only. Yours should be about your own topic.

1Claim: I argue that universities should give first-year students credit for career workshops.
2Reason: My first reason is that these workshops teach useful skills, like writing a resume.
3Other side: Some people may say that this adds too much work for students.
4My answer: However, the workshops can replace some other assignments.
5Action: So I recommend that universities make the workshops short and connected to courses.

Check: does your sentence 4 use a careful word such as may or can?

D. Say it aloud

D1Mark pauses and key words in sentence 1

I argue / that universities should give credit / for career workshops.

Any sentence with sensible pauses and 2–3 key words underlined is correct.

D2Speaking practice

Speaking only. Nothing to mark.
