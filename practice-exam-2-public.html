Lecture

Daniel Susskind, “3 myths about the future of work (and why they’re not true)”
TED@Merck KGaA, Darmstadt · 2017 · about 16 minutes

Open the talk on TED (opens in a new tab). Play it once only. No pause, rewind, or second viewing.

Section A · Discrete items · 16 marks

A1 [1]TRUE / FALSE

Susskind says the threat of technological unemployment is not real.

A2 [2]

Susskind gives two examples of machines complementing workers directly. Name the worker and the technology in each example.

A3 [1]

According to Susskind, how much bigger is the British economic “pie” today than it was 300 years ago?

A4 [3]

Where did most people in Britain work (a) 300 years ago, (b) 150 years ago, and (c) today?

A5 [2]

Name two of the three tasks that, until recently, leading economists thought could not easily be automated.

A6 [2]

Who is Richard Susskind to the speaker? In what form was Richard’s legal AI system published?

A7 [1]

How many past cases did the Stanford system analyze to decide whether a freckle is cancerous?

A8 [1]TRUE / FALSE

The Stanford system works by copying the judgment and intuition of doctors.

A9 [1]

IBM’s Watson beat two human champions on which US quiz show, and in which year?

A10 [1]

Which British economist named the “lump of labor fallacy,” and in which year?

A11 [1]

What figure does Susskind give for global GDP per head today?

Section B · Short comprehension · 12 marks

Select only the information that answers the question. Two or three accurate sentences beat a page of notes.

B1 [4]

Susskind says technology also helps workers indirectly, in two ways. Using his “pie” metaphor, explain both ways and why each one helps workers who lose tasks to machines.

B2 [4]

What is the “intelligence myth”? Explain how the routine/non-routine distinction is connected to it, and use one example from the talk to show why Susskind thinks the myth is wrong.

B3 [4]

Retell the story of the dock worker. Then explain the difference between the “lump of labor fallacy” and what Susskind calls the “superiority myth.”

Section C · Synthesis · 12 marks

C1 [12]

In 250–300 words, connect Susskind’s argument to one other source from Weeks 1–10 (a reading or a talk). Your answer must include:

  • ☐ Susskind’s central claim, stated accurately
  • ☐ The other source’s position, with author and year
  • ☐ One specific point of agreement
  • ☐ One genuine tension (a real difference, not just a different topic)
  • ☐ Your own evaluation, including one place where Susskind gives his opinion rather than evidence
