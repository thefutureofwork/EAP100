D. Ethos, pathos, logos and audience

About 10 minutes

Write 2–3 sentences for each. Use a specific moment from the talk. Your Week 8 Pause & Predict sheet can help.

D1Ethos: What makes Shen seem credible? He co-founded a hiring company. How might that also affect your judgment?

D2Pathos: Choose one personal story. What feeling might it create, and how does that help his argument?

D3Logos: Choose one claim and its support. Explain the link. What is convincing, and what is missing?

D4Audience: How might an employer react to his proposal? How might a job applicant react differently?

About 10 minutes

This is your idea. Do not present it as Shen’s.

E4Name one type of evidence that would show whether your safeguard works.

C. Write your own short argument

About 10 minutes

Choose your own future-of-work issue (you can reuse your F3 claim). Write 6–8 sentences that use all seven moves. Two moves can share one sentence.

  1. Claim: I argue that [who] should [do what].
  2. Reason: My first reason is…
  3. Evidence plan: To support this, I need evidence about…
  4. Explanation: This matters because…
  5. Objection: A reasonable objection is…
  6. Response: This concern is valid; however…
  7. Limit: This approach may help if…

Move 3 is what you still need to find. Do not invent a statistic. This is practice, not your speech script.

D. Mark it, say it, improve it

About 12 minutes

D1Choose three sentences from C. Mark pauses with / and underline the words you will stress. (Submit these marked sentences.)

Example: Employers / should use brief, job-relevant tasks / when they can provide fair access.

D2Say them aloud. Then give a 45–60 second version of your argument to a partner, or record it privately on your phone and listen back.

D3Answer aloud: “What is the strongest objection to your proposal?” Admit it, respond, and state one limit.

D4Check: could the listener hear your claim, a signpost, and a limit? Fix one thing and try once more.

D2–D4 are speaking only: nothing to write and nothing to upload. Aim for clear meaning, not a perfect accent.
