Lecture

Andrew McAfee, “What will future jobs look like?”
TED2013 · 14:59

Open the lecture on TED. Play it once only. No pause, rewind, or second viewing.

Section A · Discrete items · 16 marks

A1 [1]TRUE / FALSE

McAfee accepts that people have been predicting technology would destroy employment for about 200 years, and that they have always been wrong.

A2 [2]

According to McAfee, what makes the present moment different from earlier waves of automation?

A3 [4]

McAfee names three technologies that are displacing specific kinds of work. Give two, and state the job each one affects.

A4 [1]

What name do McAfee and Erik Brynjolfsson give to the period we are now in?

A5 [1]TRUE / FALSE

McAfee says that corporate profits and total wages paid out in the economy are both at an all-time high.

A6 [1]

Complete Reuther’s reply: “Hey Henry, how are you going to get them to …”

A7 [1]

What has happened to median income in America over the past 15 years?

A8 [4]

McAfee tells a story about two workers, Ted and Bill. Describe each in one sentence.

A9 [1]

Name one way, other than employment, in which Bill’s and Ted’s lives have diverged since 1960.

Section B · Short comprehension · 12 marks

B1 [4]

McAfee quotes Voltaire: “Work saves a man from three great evils: boredom, vice and need.” Explain why he uses this quotation at this point in the talk.

B2 [4]

McAfee says: “I’m going to start worrying about those the day my computer becomes aware of my printer.” What is he dismissing, and what is he doing rhetorically?

B3 [4]

McAfee insists the coming abundance is “not shallow materialism.” Explain the distinction he is drawing.

Section C · Synthesis · 12 marks

C1 [12]

In 200–250 words, connect McAfee’s argument to one other source from Weeks 1–5. State McAfee’s central claim accurately, state the other source’s position, identify one point of agreement and one point of genuine tension, and refer to both sources properly.
