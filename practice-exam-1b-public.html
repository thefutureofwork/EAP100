Lecture

Vlad Tenev, “AI is coming for your job. Now what?”
TEDAI San Francisco · 2025 · about 17 minutes

Open the talk on TED (opens in a new tab). Play it once only. No pause, rewind, or second viewing.

Section A · Discrete items · 16 marks

A1 [1]TRUE / FALSE

Tenev introduces himself as a historian who has studied the history of work.

A2 [2]

Tenev began his career around 2008. Name the economic event and the technology launch he connects to that year.

A3 [2]

According to Tenev, why does AI feel different from earlier tools such as the iPhone? Give two parts of his explanation.

A4 [2]

What happened to the main jobs of the Paleolithic era (hunters, gatherers, toolmakers)? Did they disappear suddenly?

A5 [2]

Name two jobs Tenev says appeared in the Neolithic era after farming and keeping livestock.

A6 [1]

What percentage of the US workforce does Tenev say farmers make up today?

A7 [1]

What job did Tenev’s great-grandfather do, and in which country?

A8 [1]

What name does Tenev give to the rapidly accelerating creation of new jobs and job families?

A9 [2]

Complete the comparison: the internet gave people ______; AI gives them ______.

A10 [1]TRUE / FALSE

Since Deep Blue beat Garry Kasparov in 1997, the chess industry has shrunk.

A11 [1]

Which career did Tenev’s teachers discourage him from in the 1990s?

Section B · Short comprehension · 12 marks

B1 [4]

Tenev suggests that what really makes people nervous is not job disruption itself. What is it, and how does his tour through history respond to that worry?

B2 [4]

Tenev claims that future jobs “will not look like real work.” Explain this claim, give one example he uses from today’s jobs, and say what the humor in this section does for his argument.

B3 [4]

Near the end, Tenev tells two short stories: one about his teachers’ career advice and one about Deep Blue and chess. What point do both stories make, and what advice does he give the audience?

Section C · Synthesis · 12 marks

C1 [12]

In 200–250 words, connect Tenev’s argument to one other source from Weeks 1–5. State Tenev’s central claim accurately, state the other source’s position, identify one point of agreement and one point of genuine tension, and refer to both sources properly.
